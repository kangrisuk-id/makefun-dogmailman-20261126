# Candidates — The Dog Reviews the Mailman

Judge brief criteria (in order, most important first):
- **C1** — funny one-line review a dog gives the mailman is the centre (on-topic gate)
- **C2** — the dog's side of the visit is the joke: grievance, bribe, suspicion, small triumph
- **C3** — voice is a dog's, with a dog's priorities and vocabulary
- **C4** — one line, tight and complete, with a turn at the end that earns the laugh
- **C5** — the laugh comes from this dog and this mailman, not a pun for any stranger at the door

Each criterion /5. Total /25. All lines < 200 chars.

## Knockout tournament

### Quarter-finals
- **QF1: #7 vs #12** — Both sniff-the-bag lines. C1 tie. C2: #7's suspicion (never allowed to sniff) edges #12's grievance. C3 tie (5/5). C4: #12's "lousy envelope" turn is punchier than #7's flat ending. C5: #7's "1,500 visits" is more specific. Decided on C2+C5: **#7 wins, narrowly.**
- **QF2: #14 vs #30** — C3: #30's "smells of other dogs' houses" is peak dog voice; #14's threat is peak triumph. C4: #14 has the double turn ("keeping you alive" + "You're welcome") vs #30's single "without petting me". C5: #30 slightly more specific. Decided on C4: **#14 wins.**
- **QF3: #22 vs #25** — C2 tie (suspicion vs triumph). C4: #25's "I have never felt more professional" lands a bigger laugh than #22's long suspicion sentence. C3: #22's dog-logic is superb, but #25's clipped "Not walked — ran" is pure dog excitement. Decided on C4: **#25 wins.**
- **QF4: #24 vs #26** — Both small triumphs, both 5 stars. C4: #26's reversal (the dog trained the mailman) is a cleverer turn than #24's "promotion". C5: #26's "six years of barking" is more specific. **#26 wins.**

### Semi-finals
- **SF1: #7 vs #14** — C1–C3 tie. C4: #14's double turn is the funniest single ending in the field; #7 ends flatly on "One star". C2: #14's threat/triumph beats #7's suspicion. **#14 wins.**
- **SF2: #25 vs #26** — Both triumphs. C4: #26's reversal edges #25's "more professional" image. C5: #26's six-year history is more specific to this pair. **#26 wins, narrowly.**

### Final: #14 vs #26
- C1: tie (5/5, both dead-centre dog reviews of the mailman).
- C2: tie-ish — #14 threat/triumph vs #26 triumph; #14's threat is bolder.
- C3: tie (5/5) — #14's magnanimous "You're welcome" vs #26's dog-logic "the training is paying off".
- C4: **#14 wins** — two turns in one line ("keeping you alive", then "You're welcome") vs #26's one reversal.
- C5: #26 slightly ahead (six years), but not by more than #14's C4 margin.
- **Winner: #14.**
